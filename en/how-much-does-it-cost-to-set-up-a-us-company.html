How much does it cost to set up a US company from China?

Short answer: For a Chinese company, registering a US company and running it through the first year commonly costs $3,000–$10,000 in total. Registration itself is only a small part — compliance, banking, and tax filing are where the money goes.

First-year cost breakdown

Cost itemTypical rangeNotes
State filing fee$50–$500Varies by state; Delaware LLC $90, Wyoming $100, California $70
Registered agent$100–$300/yearRequired in most states; must have an in-state address
EIN (federal tax ID)$0Free from the IRS website — never pay an agent a premium for it
Bank account opening$0–$500Opening itself is usually free; travel and document preparation are the real cost, and approval is the bank's decision
First-year tax filing (CPA)$1,000–$3,000Depends on entity type and transaction volume; even a zero return has a cost
Franchise tax / annual report$0–$800Delaware franchise tax starts at $175; annual report fees are separate
Business address / virtual office$100–$1,200/yearDepends on whether you need a physical address for mail

Three steps

  1. Choose the state, then the entity type: LLC or C-Corp, and which state to register in, depend on your business and tax goals. Get a recommendation first, then file.
  2. Registration + EIN + bank account: Registration takes 2–4 weeks; apply for the EIN, then start the bank account process, usually within 2–6 weeks after registration.
  3. Set up a first-year compliance calendar first: Annual reports, franchise tax, and tax filing deadlines on one sheet — missing a deadline often costs more in penalties than the registration fee itself.

Case example

A Chinese home-goods factory registered a Wyoming LLC in 2024 (filing fee: $100), expecting the whole thing to cost $500. Actual first-year spending came to about $6,000: $1,800 for CPA tax filing, $400 for the registered agent and address, and about $2,000 in travel and notarization for the bank account opening. Lesson: the filing fee is the cheapest part. Budget by total first-year cost, not by the filing fee.

Common mistakes

  • "Once registered, we can operate." Wrong. Without an EIN you cannot open a bank account, and without a bank account you cannot receive payments.
  • "A zero return costs nothing." Wrong. A zero return still goes through the filing process, and CPA fees and state annual reports still apply.
  • "The cheapest state is the cheapest option." Not necessarily. A cheap registration state can mean extra registration and tax costs in the state where you actually operate. Count both ends.
